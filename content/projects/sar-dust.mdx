Work done at the Sensors Directorate Internship Program with NASIC mentors, exploring how noise attacks degrade
Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) image classification and how to make CNN classifiers more
robust to it.

We benchmarked a ResNet-18 trained on clean SAR imagery (97.14% accuracy, no noise)
against 9 different noise models inlcuding dependent (multiplicative Gaussian, Poisson), independent
(Gaussian, Rayleigh, Uniform, Erlang), and structural (Brownian, periodic, salt-and-pepper) models
at varying intensity (PSNR). The model generalizes well to light noise but collapses onto a
single predicted class once PSNR drops to roughly 10-15 dB, a result that held consistently
across all 9 noise models.

To improve robustness, we applied a bilateral speckle filter to isolate targets from
background clutter, using an adaptive threshold (`T = mean + 2*stddev`) on the smoothed pixel
distribution. Mixing these isolated-target samples 50/50 into training produced a model that
matched baseline accuracy on clean data (97.93%) while handling noisy inputs far better --
up to 66.7% higher accuracy than the original model at PSNR 13 dB. Guided Grad-CAM saliency
maps show why: the 50/50 model shifts attention off of target shadows and onto the target
shape itself. Thus our conclusion is that we can increase model robustness by simply inducing shape bias within the model.
