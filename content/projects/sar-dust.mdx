This work was done at the Sensors Directorate Internship Program with NASIC mentors. We explore how noise attacks degrade Synthetic
Aperture Radar (SAR) image classifcation and how to make CNN classifiers more robust to it. 


We benchmarked a ResNet-18 trained on clean SAR imagery (97.14% accuracy, no noise)
against 9 different noise models inlcuding dependent (multiplicative Gaussian, Poisson), independent
(Gaussian, Rayleigh, Uniform, Erlang), and structural (Brownian, periodic, salt-and-pepper) noise models
at varying intensity (PSNR). The model generalizes well to light noise but collapses onto a
single predicted class once PSNR drops to roughly 10-15 dB, a result that held consistently
across all 9 noise models.

To improve robustness, we add isolated targets to the training set.
To isolate targets, we first apply a bilateral speckle filter, and use an adaptive threshold 
(`T = mean + 2*stddev`) on the smoothed pixel
distribution. Mixing these isolated-target samples 50/50 into training produced a model that
matched baseline accuracy on clean data (97.93%) while handling noisy inputs far better. Our best result was a 66.7% gain 
in classification accuracy at around 13 dB of PSNR. 
We compute Guided Grad-CAM saliency
maps to help explain why. We see from the plots that the 50/50 model shifts attention off of target shadows and onto the target
shape itself. Thus our conclusion is that we can increase model robustness by simply inducing shape bias within the model.
